Optical Cryptography

How Visual Cryptography Works

No computer chips required. Decoding relies entirely on the human visual system.

01

1. Discrete Pixels

The original image is decomposed into discrete binary pixels (or halftoned CMY subtractive ink dots for full colour).

02

2. Floyd-Steinberg Halftoning

Continuous-tone images are transformed via error-diffusion dithering, preserving tonal depth with binary ink distribution.

03
White: Share 1 = Share 2
Black: Share 2 = Complement

3. 2×2 Subpixel Expansion

Each pixel becomes four subpixels with exactly two inked positions chosen from 6 permutations with cryptographically secure randomness.

04
SEALED

4. Superposition Reveal

Stacking the transparent sheets computes a physical logical OR / per-channel MIN. White pixels reflect 50% light; black pixels block 100%.

Mathematical Formulations

Supported Cryptographic Schemes

From physical transparent overlays to digital lossless XOR sharing.

Mode A · New v2

Colour Overlay 2-out-of-2

P5 · Hou 2003

Decomposes RGB into Cyan, Magenta, and Yellow subtractive ink channels with independent Floyd-Steinberg halftoning. Physical stacking acts as subtractive ink multiplication (per-channel min), and display enhancement reconstructs vibrant continuous colour.

  • Decoding: Physical transparency overlay (no computer needed)
  • Expansion: 2×2 subpixels (4× pixel area)
  • Security: 0-bit single-share information leakage
  • Resolution: Enhanced CMY recovery + Gaussian smoothing

Mode B · New v2

Colour XOR (n, n) Secret Sharing

P6 · Wang et al. 2005

Full 24-bit RGB secret sharing across n shares ($2 \le n \le 6$) using cryptographically secure bitwise XOR. Zero pixel expansion and byte-for-byte exact lossless reconstruction upon combining all shares.

  • Decoding: Digital computation (Bitwise XOR)
  • Expansion: 1:1 (zero pixel expansion)
  • Security: Information-theoretically secure for any $n-1$ shares
  • Fidelity: Byte-for-byte exact colour match (PSNR = ∞)

Baseline P1

B/W Overlay 2-out-of-2

P1 · Naor & Shamir 1994

The seminal visual cryptography scheme. A binary secret is divided into two transparencies with 2×2 subpixel expansion. Stacking achieves a 50% contrast differential without any calculation.

  • Decoding: Pure optical transparency superposition
  • Expansion: 2×2 subpixels
  • Contrast: Relative difference = 0.5 (exact theory)
  • Benchmark: Proven historical standard

Digital P6

B/W XOR (n, n)

P6 · Wang 2005

Binary secret sharing across up to 6 participants using bitwise modular arithmetic. Produces compact binary shares that restore the secret with 100% contrast.

  • Decoding: Digital bitwise XOR
  • Expansion: 1:1 (zero expansion)
  • Contrast: 1.0 (perfect black and white)
  • Multi-party: Supports 2 to 6 shares

Practical Deployments

Real-World Use Cases

Where visual cryptography safeguards sensitive information across decentralized architectures.

Biometric Privacy Protection (P7)

Facial recognition templates and fingerprint minutiae images are divided into two non-communicating cloud databases. Even a complete database breach exposes zero biometric data; the template is only reconstructed during live authentication.

Tamper-Proof Online Voting (P8)

Voter ballot confirmations are split between the voter's physical receipt and the election audit ledger. Neither election officials nor receipt handlers can alter or inspect individual votes without the voter's physical share.

Medical Imagery & Telehealth

High-resolution diagnostic medical scans and patient identification are separated into distinct cryptographic shares. Complies with HIPAA/GDPR by ensuring that data-in-transit carries zero intelligible diagnostic artifacts if intercepted.
